## 3.3 Signal across molecular and clinical subgroups (univariable)

Section 3.2 pooled patients. This section asks whether the DTP-to-outcome association holds inside molecular and clinical strata, and whether the DTP score itself varies across those strata. Both questions reuse the univariable tests from 3.2, now run within CMS subtypes (CMS1 to CMS4), PDS subtypes (PDS1 to PDS3 and the mixed class), stage groups, and microsatellite status (MSI and MSS), in GSE39582 and TCGA-COAD. One caveat governs every cell. Splitting a few hundred patients into subtypes leaves most strata with few events, so a non-significant or not-testable subgroup marks a limit of power rather than evidence that the association is absent there. The figure draws strata that fail the events-per-variable gate as not testable, kept visually distinct from strata that were tested and returned nothing.

Within GSE39582 the Up score reached significance in the larger strata and in the microsatellite-instable group, where the effect ran largest. The MSI group carried the highest per-SD hazard ratio at both endpoints (OS HR 2.08, 95% CI 1.26 to 3.41, FDR = 0.026; RFS HR 2.56, 1.40 to 4.67, FDR = 0.016, on 74 patients), and the microsatellite-stable group reached recurrence significance on its larger sample (RFS HR 1.28, 1.08 to 1.51, FDR = 0.021), with overall survival at the threshold (OS HR 1.31, 1.06 to 1.61, FDR = 0.05). Among CMS subtypes only CMS4 reached overall-survival significance (HR 1.62, 1.20 to 2.19, FDR = 0.007), the mesenchymal subtype where a persister-stress programme is most expected. PDS2 showed the same for overall survival (Up HR 1.59, 1.13 to 2.25, FDR = 0.036; Composite HR 1.79, 1.28 to 2.52, FDR = 0.007), and stage III carried a clear overall-survival signal (HR 1.84, 1.36 to 2.51, FDR = 0.001). The Composite score followed the Up score in each of these strata. The Down score reached significance once, protective in PDS2 overall survival (HR 0.66, 0.48 to 0.91, FDR = 0.037), which sits within the noise expected from many subgroup tests.

TCGA-COAD reached significance in one stratum, CMS1 recurrence (Up HR 1.81, 1.21 to 2.69, FDR = 0.034). The rest returned nothing after correction, in line with the smaller event counts. The point estimates still pointed the way 3.2 predicts: CMS4, PDS2, stage III and the microsatellite-stable group each gave an Up-score hazard ratio above one (1.27 to 1.42), with confidence intervals crossing one. Direction agreed across cohorts even where the TCGA subgroups lacked the events to confirm it.

The second question, whether the DTP score itself differs across subtypes, has a clear answer from the pairwise comparisons between subtype levels (Figure 7; full statistics in Appendix Table A7). The Up score separated subtypes most strongly across the PDS axis: in GSE39582 the regenerative PDS2 carried the highest score and PDS1 the lowest, every PDS pair was significant, and the PDS1-versus-PDS2 gap was the largest anywhere (rank-biserial r = 0.78), a pattern TCGA-COAD reproduced (PDS1 versus PDS2 r = 0.58, PDS1 versus PDS3 r = 0.51). Across CMS the mesenchymal CMS4 held the highest score and CMS2 the lowest, with the CMS2-versus-CMS4 contrast the strongest (r = 0.70 in GSE39582, r = 0.36 in TCGA-COAD) and five of six CMS pairs significant in GSE39582. The Composite score followed the same ordering at slightly lower effect, while the Down score did not separate the CMS subtypes in either cohort and moved only weakly across PDS. The subtypes carrying the highest persister score, CMS4 and PDS2, are the same mesenchymal and regenerative classes in which the Up score was prognostic earlier in this section, so the programme is elevated in exactly the subtypes where a persister-stress state is most expected. A score that tracks both outcome and subtype raises the prospect that subtype confounds the primary association, which the multivariable analysis in Section 3.4 tests directly.

The subgroup breakdown supports the specificity of the primary signal rather than diluting it. The Up score reached significance where events were plentiful, as in the large microsatellite-stable group, where the effect ran largest, as in the smaller microsatellite-instable group, and where a persister-stress programme is biologically most expected, namely the mesenchymal CMS4, PDS2 and stage III strata of GSE39582; it held one direction across cohorts and stayed on the Up and Composite axes. The strata that returned nothing were the small ones. These subgroup results should be read as exploratory, and a sensitivity check makes the point concrete: when the false-discovery correction is pooled across all 159 testable subgroup tests, rather than applied within each stratum, only one association survives (stage III overall survival in GSE39582, q = 0.015), against fourteen under the per-stratum scheme. The subgroup analysis is therefore hypothesis-generating and does not, on its own, establish effect in any particular stratum; its value is in showing that the primary signal is present where events are plentiful and where persister biology is expected, not diluted by it. Figure 6 shows the per-subgroup hazard ratios, Figure 7 shows the score distributions across subtype in both cohorts with pairwise comparisons, and Appendix Table A1 lists every subgroup with its events, testability, hazard ratio and corrected p-value. That the score also varies across subtype leaves open whether its prognostic value is genuinely its own or a restatement of subtype, stage or microsatellite status, which the next section tests directly.
