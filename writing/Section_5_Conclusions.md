# 5. Conclusions and Future Directions

## 5.1 Conclusions

This thesis tested whether a transcriptomic drug-tolerant persister programme behaves as a genuine cancer-stress state in patient tumours, using a single scoring approach applied to three independent predictions. The work supports a clear and bounded conclusion.

What can be concluded is that the up-regulated persister programme is a genuine prognostic signal in primary colorectal cancer, and that the signal is its own rather than a restatement of established biology. The association replicated across two cohorts and two platforms, was clearest for recurrence and in the adjuvant-treated subset, and survived adjustment for stage, microsatellite status and both molecular-subtype taxonomies. It sat specifically on the persister and fetal-regenerative axis, since the fetal intestinal programme tracked outcome alongside it while generic stemness and proliferation programmes did not, and the MYC module ran the other way. That the signal was strongest under chemotherapy is the behaviour a drug-tolerant state should show, and it converges with an independently derived slow-cycling colorectal subtype that also marks poor outcome. On the primary prediction, and on the requirement that the score add information beyond the standard taxonomy, the programme behaves as a real state and not as an arbitrary gene list.

What cannot be concluded is equally important to state. The programme is not a universal pan-cancer stress marker: across the solid-tumour atlas its prognostic value was weak overall and concentrated in a minority of tissues, notably low-grade glioma and pancreatic adenocarcinoma, whose plasticity-driven biology fits a persister interpretation but whose strength does not license a general claim. And the prediction that the programme is enriched in the metastatic compartment was neither confirmed nor refuted: the up programme showed no enrichment in bulk metastasis even when adequately measured, but a bulk score dilutes a state carried by only a fraction of cells and so cannot resolve a signal confined to that fraction. The metastasis question is therefore left open rather than answered in the negative. Taken together, the evidence is a partial validation: the programme is a real, specific and independent prognostic marker in treated colorectal cancer, of context-dependent value elsewhere, and of unproven but not disproven relevance to metastasis.

## 5.2 Future directions

The most direct next step follows from where the signal was strongest. Because the association was clearest in adjuvant-treated patients, the priority is external validation in an independent, uniformly treated colorectal cohort with recorded recurrence, which would test the confirmatory result in the setting where it is most likely to be clinically meaningful and would establish whether the score adds to standard prognostic variables in prospective use.

The metastasis question should be reopened at the resolution the biology requires. A bulk paired design cannot separate a sparse cell-state signal from the tissue average, so single-cell or spatial transcriptomics of matched primary and metastatic tumours is the appropriate test, and it would also show directly whether the down, revival and regenerative programmes that moved in the bulk metastasis analysis reflect distinct cell populations.

The tissue-specific pan-cancer leads are worth pursuing directly rather than treating as incidental. The strength of the signal in low-grade glioma and pancreatic adenocarcinoma is a concrete, testable hypothesis: that the persister and fetal-regenerative programme marks the plasticity-driven aggressive fraction of these tumours. Testing it would mean examining the score against treatment and outcome within these tissues specifically, ideally with the same independence checks used here, and separating a genuine persister effect from the wide prognostic spread that inflates discrimination in low-grade glioma.

Two methodological items should be settled before the analysis is considered final. The false-discovery correction family should be fixed against an explicit count of the genuinely independent primary tests, so that the confirmatory claims are corrected as confirmatory tests rather than diluted against the exploratory set or, conversely, under-corrected; the position taken here is defensible but the borderline results remain sensitive to it. And the input scale of the pathway-derived subtype classifier should be confirmed, so that the subtype-adjusted results rest on correctly called subtypes.

Finally, the persister interpretation invites experimental follow-up beyond association. The signal was defined from a colorectal persister model, and the natural test of the hypothesis is functional: whether tumours or patient-derived models scoring high for the programme are in fact enriched for drug-tolerant, slow-cycling cells under chemotherapy, and whether targeting the state changes the likelihood of relapse. Such work would move the programme from a prognostic marker, which this thesis supports it to be in colorectal cancer, toward a causal account of the relapse it is intended to explain.
