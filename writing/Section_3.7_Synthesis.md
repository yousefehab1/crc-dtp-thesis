## 3.7 Synthesis of results

One signature, scored the same way in every dataset, was tested first for its clinical value in colorectal cancer and then, exploratorily, in other tissues and in metastasis (Figure 3). The main result is clear: in primary colorectal cancer the DTP up programme tracked outcome in two independently processed cohorts, clearest for recurrence in the whole cohort and strongest for overall survival in the adjuvant-treated subset, and it added prognostic information beyond stage, microsatellite status and molecular subtype. In other words, the cell-line-derived signature carries independent prognostic value in colorectal cancer patients, which was the primary aim.

The two exploratory strands added texture but not a second headline. Across the 25 solid-tumour cohorts the score leaned in the same direction, toward higher hazard, but reached significance in only a few; where it was strong it was strong specifically in low-grade glioma and pancreatic adenocarcinoma, and it was weak or absent elsewhere and even reversed in papillary kidney cancer, so the signature is prognostic in particular tissues rather than across cancer in general. In the metastatic compartment, once liver contamination was controlled, the up programme showed no enrichment in bulk, while the programmes that did move were led by Myc and by proliferation- and stemness-related sets. The overall message is that the signature's clinical value is established in colorectal cancer, is tissue-specific rather than universal when looked for elsewhere, and is neither confirmed nor excluded in metastasis by a bulk analysis of this size. The Discussion weighs what this means.
