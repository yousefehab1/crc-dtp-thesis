## 3.6 Enrichment in metastasis

The second exploratory question is whether the persister programme is enriched in metastatic tissue. This is asked tentatively rather than as a firm prediction, because persister cells are dormant and slow-cycling whereas metastasis is driven by disseminating, proliferating cells, so any persister contribution might be confined to the earliest seeding of a metastasis and absent from an established lesion. This section tests that in GSE50760, paired RNA-seq of matched normal mucosa, primary tumour and liver metastasis from the same patients, roughly 18 patients. Differential expression used a paired limma design with patient as a blocking factor on log2(FPKM+1), giving two contrasts from one fit, normal versus primary and primary versus metastasis, and signature enrichment was read from GSEA on the ranked differential-expression statistic. Because liver metastases carry adjacent hepatic tissue, a naive primary-versus-metastasis comparison confounds tumour biology with liver contamination, so a liver-specific gene score was carried as a covariate and the metastasis contrast was adjusted for it.

The contamination is real and it changes the result. The liver score was low and flat across the normal and primary samples, near 0.1, and rose sharply in the metastases, to a median near 0.7 and approaching 1 in most patients with a few staying low (Figure 12A). Adjusting the primary-versus-metastasis ranking for that score moved the enrichment rather than leaving it untouched (Figure 12B). Adjustment sharpened the genuine tumour signals and removed spurious ones: the Myc programme was up in the metastasis before and after and strengthened (NES +2.58 to +3.60), the DTP down set strengthened (+1.39 to +1.63), and the revSC, regenerative stem cell and fetal intestinal programmes emerged as significant metastasis enrichments only once the liver signal was removed (revSC +0.96 to +1.91; regenerative +1.22 to +1.39; fetal intestinal -1.08 to +1.29). In the other direction the crypt-base columnar cell (CBC) programme lost significance (+1.30 to -0.93) and the IBD programme, significantly higher in the primary before adjustment, went null (-1.44 to +0.99). The DTP up score stayed non-significant throughout (-0.95 to +1.08). The metastasis contrast is therefore read from the adjusted ranking throughout.

Along the normal-to-primary-to-metastasis axis the programmes that moved were proliferative and stem-like (Figure 13). From normal to primary, where neither arm contains liver and no adjustment is needed, every scored programme except the DTP up score was up in the primary tumour, positive normalised enrichment scores here denoting up in primary: Myc most strongly (NES +3.51, FDR 5e-56), followed by the revSC (+1.86), crypt-base columnar cell (CBC) (+1.81), IBD (+1.65), fetal intestinal (+1.55), DTP down (+1.38) and regenerative stem cell (+1.35) programmes; the DTP up score was not significant (-1.03, FDR 0.34). From primary to metastasis, on the purity-adjusted ranking, the Myc (+3.60, FDR 1e-47), revSC (+1.91, FDR 7e-9), DTP down (+1.63, FDR 2e-5), regenerative stem cell (+1.39, FDR 0.015) and fetal intestinal (+1.29, FDR 0.033) programmes were up in the metastasis, and the DTP up score was not significant (+1.08, FDR 0.30).

The DTP up programme therefore showed no enrichment in the metastatic compartment, in either direction and after removing the liver confound, so this dataset does not support the metastasis prediction at the bulk level. Two features of the result are worth stating plainly. The DTP down set, which carried no outcome signal in Section 3.2, was among the programmes enriched in the metastasis, alongside the Myc and the stem-like revival and regenerative scores. And the fetal intestinal programme, which tracked outcome with the DTP up score in Section 3.2, was itself enriched in the metastasis, whereas the DTP up score was not, so the two axes that moved together in the primary tumour separated in the metastatic compartment. What these two figures establish is that the contamination confound is real and controlled, and that the up-regulated persister programme is not differentially abundant between a primary tumour and its matched liver metastasis in bulk. The interpretation of that null is taken up in the Discussion. With the three predictions now tested, the strands can be drawn together at the level of evidence.
