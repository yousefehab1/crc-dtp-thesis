## 3.4 Independence from clinicopathology (multivariable)

Sections 3.2 and 3.3 leave one question open. The DTP score tracks outcome, and it also varies across subtype (Section 3.3), so its prognostic value could restate stage, microsatellite status or molecular subtype rather than add information of its own. This section tests independence three ways: a Cox model that adjusts the continuous score for clinicopathology, here stage and microsatellite status, the prognostic variables recorded consistently across both cohorts, and measures how far the score's effect shifts, a likelihood-ratio test of whether the score improves fit beyond those covariates, and an interaction model that asks whether the score's effect differs across subgroups. The raw per-SD hazard ratios keep the narrow-range problem from 3.2, so the text reports the likelihood-ratio p-value, the change in the score's log hazard ratio after adjustment, and the model concordance.

In GSE39582 the Up score improved fit beyond a clinicopathological model of stage and microsatellite status at both endpoints (overall survival likelihood-ratio p = 0.004, FDR = 0.009; recurrence p = 0.004, FDR = 0.013). Adjustment shifted the score's log hazard ratio by 13% for overall survival and 17% for recurrence, so stage and microsatellite status accounted for little of the association, and the adjusted models reached concordance 0.63 for overall survival and 0.67 for recurrence. The score held its overall-survival value after adjustment for CMS (FDR = 0.033) but not after PDS (FDR = 0.122); for recurrence the pattern reversed, with the score surviving PDS adjustment (FDR = 0.039) and weakening under CMS (FDR = 0.077). PDS absorbed more of the overall-survival signal than CMS or clinicopathology, in line with the larger score-across-subtype effect for PDS in Section 3.3. The Composite score followed the Up score, significant beyond clinicopathology at both endpoints and beyond CMS for overall survival.

The adjuvant-treated subset showed the score's independence most plainly. The Up score improved fit beyond clinicopathology for overall survival (p = 0.003, FDR = 0.009) and recurrence (p = 0.005, FDR = 0.014), and adjustment shifted its log hazard ratio by under 2%, so measured clinicopathology explained almost none of the treated-subset association. The subtype-adjusted models in this subset lost significance after correction, which follows from splitting 240 patients further rather than from the score losing effect.

TCGA-COAD supported the same independence where the sample allowed it. The clinicopathology-adjusted model there rested on 198 patients with complete stage and treatment records and did not reach significance (overall survival p = 0.13). Adjusting instead for CMS, on the larger 425-patient sample, the Up score improved fit for both overall survival (FDR = 0.041) and recurrence (FDR = 0.040), and survived PDS adjustment for overall survival as well (FDR = 0.046). The Composite score reached significance in no adjusted TCGA model.

The interaction models asked whether the score's effect changes across subgroups. Effect modification appeared in one place, overall survival in GSE39582, where the Up and Composite scores interacted with both CMS (Up FDR = 0.012, Composite FDR = 0.030) and PDS (Up FDR = 0.039, Composite FDR = 0.019), and the Down score interacted with PDS (FDR = 0.011). No interaction reached significance for recurrence, for stage or microsatellite status, in the treated subset, or in TCGA-COAD. The strong within-MSI association from Section 3.3 does not surface as a microsatellite interaction here, because the Up score raised hazard in both the MSI and MSS strata, so their slopes did not differ enough for the interaction test to detect. The per-subgroup forest shows the source of the overall-survival interaction: the Up hazard ratio ran above one in CMS1 and CMS4 and near or below one in CMS2 and CMS3, so the score carried its prognostic weight in the immune and mesenchymal subtypes rather than evenly across all four. These interaction tests are low-powered, so the many null results mark limited power rather than proven uniformity.

The score therefore adds prognostic information beyond stage, microsatellite status and molecular subtype in GSE39582, and beyond CMS and, for overall survival, PDS in TCGA-COAD, with only modest attenuation and with its clearest independence in the adjuvant-treated subset. Molecular subtype, PDS in particular, absorbs part of the overall-survival signal, and the score's overall-survival effect is stronger in some subtypes than others. Adjustment removes only measured confounders, so residual confounding remains possible, a limitation the Discussion takes up. Figure 8 summarises how far the score's effect changes after each adjustment, Figure 9 shows the interaction map and the per-subgroup forest, and the full adjusted and interaction tables are reported as Appendix Tables A2 and A3. Having shown the signal to be an independent prognostic marker within colorectal cancer, the second prediction asks whether it is peculiar to this tissue or holds across cancer types.
