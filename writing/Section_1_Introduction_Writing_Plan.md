# Section 1 — Introduction: writing plan

Purpose: motivate the single hypothesis and its three predictions, and set up the computational approach the thesis rests on. The emphasis is deliberately weighted toward method and reasoning rather than deep molecular biology. Enough biology is given to justify why a drug-tolerant persister programme is worth measuring and why each gene set is in the panel, but the centre of gravity is the signature-scoring approach, the choice of gene sets, and the cross-dataset design. This is where examiner criterion 2 is earned, so the gap, hypothesis and aims at the end must be explicit.

Target about 3,800 words. Suggested weighting given the methods lean: 1.1 short (about 450), 1.2 moderate (about 550), 1.3 heavy (about 800), 1.4 heavy (about 900), 1.5 short (about 450), 1.6 explicit and tight (about 650). This is the reverse of a biology-first introduction: the two longest subsections are the scoring method and the gene-set reasoning.

House style: prose, no em dashes, past or present tense as appropriate for a review, positive framing of design choices.

## 1.1 Colorectal cancer and the problem of relapse

Short. Establish the clinical problem the thesis speaks to: colorectal cancer burden, the role of adjuvant chemotherapy (5-fluorouracil-based) in stage II and III disease, and the fact that a substantial fraction of treated patients still relapse. Frame relapse after cytotoxic therapy as the unsolved problem, so a transcriptional state that could seed relapse becomes worth measuring. Keep the epidemiology brief; do not over-invest here.

## 1.2 The drug-tolerant persister state

Moderate. Define the drug-tolerant persister (DTP) phenotype: a reversible, slow-cycling, non-mutational tolerance state that a subpopulation of tumour cells enters under cytotoxic pressure and can exit once pressure is removed, making it a candidate substrate for relapse. Review the persister concept across cancer types briefly (its discovery in targeted-therapy settings and its generalisation), then narrow to the colorectal and 5-FU context that the DTP gene set was derived from. The key conceptual point to plant for later: a persister state is a cell-state property, often carried by a minority of cells, which sets up both the rationale for a transcriptional signature and the later caveat that bulk measurement dilutes it (returned to in the Discussion).

## 1.3 Transcriptomic signatures and single-sample scoring (methods-leaning core)

Heavy. This subsection carries the computational argument.
- Why signature-based approaches: a biological state is better captured by the coordinated behaviour of a gene set than by any single marker, which is robust to platform noise and to individual gene dropout.
- Why single-sample, rank-based scoring: introduce ssGSEA conceptually (without method detail, which is in Methods) as a within-sample rank statistic that asks whether a set's genes are coordinately high in a given sample. State its two properties that matter for this design: it is computed per sample, so every patient gets a continuous score usable in survival models; and it depends only on within-sample ranks, so it is invariant to monotonic transforms and comparable across microarray and RNA-seq once each is on a consistent scale.
- Why a continuous score and landmark survival rather than a marker-positive cut: continuous scoring avoids an arbitrary threshold and preserves power; a landmark converts survival into a clean classification at a fixed horizon.
- Why triangulation across independent datasets: one association in one cohort is weak evidence; the design tests the same programme in independent cohorts and on independent platforms, and against different biological questions (primary outcome, cross-tissue, metastasis), so agreement across them is the evidence. This paragraph sets up the three-prediction structure without yet stating the hypothesis.
- Brief, honest note on the limits of the approach: a bulk single-sample score measures an average over all cells, and signature scores are relative within a sample, so consistent normalisation matters. These are stated plainly here and expanded in the Discussion.

## 1.4 The gene sets and why they were chosen (methods-leaning core)

Heavy, and the subsection the request most wants. Present the panel and the reasoning, ideally with the gene-set table below. The argument has two parts.

First, the DTP programme itself: the up-regulated set (Up) and the down-regulated set (Down) that define the persister transcriptional state, and the Composite defined as Up minus Down, which sharpens the contrast by combining the two directions into one score. The Up and Down sets are an in-house signature generated by the Atlasi Laboratory and are not yet published, so they are described as a laboratory-derived persister signature (Atlasi Laboratory, unpublished) rather than cited to an external source. State briefly how they were derived (persister model, up- and down-regulated components) at the level the lab is comfortable disclosing.

Second, and this is the reasoning the section must make explicit, the six reference signatures are not incidental: they were chosen so that the specificity of any DTP result could be judged against the neighbouring biology, because a persister state overlaps with stemness, regeneration and proliferation programmes and a prognostic signal that is merely a restatement of generic stemness would be far less interesting. The reference sets span the axes a sceptic would raise:
- Foetal stem cell and the revival and regenerative stem cell programmes (Foetal, revCSC, regStemCell) capture the foetal-like, YAP-associated regenerative reprogramming that injured and malignant intestine re-enter, the closest biological neighbour to a persister state.
- The colorectal cancer stem cell programme (CSC) captures canonical Lgr5-type intestinal stemness.
- The inflammatory bowel disease regeneration programme (IBD) captures an inflammation-driven regenerative state.
- The MYC target module (Myc) captures proliferation, the obvious alternative explanation for any outcome association.
The reasoning to state: if the DTP up score tracks outcome while these related programmes largely do not, the signal is specific to the persister and foetal-regenerative axis rather than to stemness or proliferation in general, which is exactly the comparison Section 3.2 reports. Note the coverage gate here as a one-line methodological point: a set is only scored where enough of its genes are present, which matters for the small revCSC set.

## 1.5 Molecular subtypes as confounders and effect modifiers

Short, methods-oriented. Introduce the consensus molecular subtypes (CMS) and pathway-derived subtypes (PDS), microsatellite instability and stage, but only to the depth needed to justify their later use. The point to make is not a full review of CRC taxonomy but the methodological one: these are established prognostic axes, they are correlated with stemness and regeneration biology, and so any claim that the DTP score is prognostic must show that it is not simply re-expressing subtype, stage or microsatellite status. This motivates the adjusted and interaction analyses of Section 3.4 and the score-across-subtype test of Section 3.3.

## 1.6 Gap, hypothesis and aims

Explicit and tight. State the gap: the DTP programme is defined largely from in vitro or short-term models, and whether it behaves as a genuine cancer-relevant state in patient tumours, as opposed to a colorectal-specific or artefactual gene list, has not been tested across the independent lines of evidence that would distinguish the two. State the single hypothesis: that a transcriptomic DTP programme, if it is a real cancer-stress state, should make three independent and directionally consistent predictions. State the three predictions and their aims, mapped to the three modules: it should track outcome in primary colorectal cancer; it should generalise across cancer types; and it should be enriched in the metastatic compartment. Close by noting that the three predictions are tested with the shared scoring approach of 1.3 and the panel of 1.4, and point forward to the study-design figure in the Results.
