# Abstract

Relapse after chemotherapy is the event that most often precedes death from colorectal cancer, and it can occur even when a tumour has responded and no resistance mutation is present. One explanation is the drug-tolerant persister state, a reversible, slow-cycling phenotype that a fraction of tumour cells enter under cytotoxic pressure and later exit to regrow. This thesis asked whether a transcriptomic drug-tolerant persister programme behaves as a genuine cancer-stress state in patient tumours, or is instead a colorectal-specific or artefactual gene list.

An in-house persister signature (up-regulated, down-regulated and composite scores) was scored in every sample by single-sample gene set enrichment analysis, alongside six reference programmes spanning stemness, regeneration and proliferation so that the specificity of any result could be judged. The signature was tested against three predictions that a real stress state should satisfy: that it should track outcome in primary colorectal cancer, generalise across cancer types, and be enriched in metastasis. These were examined in two primary colorectal cohorts (GSE39582 and TCGA-COAD), twenty-five solid-tumour cohorts from The Cancer Genome Atlas, and a paired normal, primary and metastasis dataset, using landmark survival models with adjustment for clinicopathology and molecular subtype, and liver-adjusted differential expression for the metastasis contrast.

The three predictions returned three verdicts. In primary colorectal cancer the up score tracked overall survival and recurrence, replicated across both platforms, was strongest in adjuvant-treated patients, and remained significant after adjustment for stage, microsatellite status and molecular subtype. The signal was specific: it lay on the persister and fetal-regenerative axis, since a fetal intestinal programme tracked outcome alongside it while generic stemness and proliferation programmes did not. Across cancer types the association was weak overall but strong in a minority of tissues, notably low-grade glioma and pancreatic adenocarcinoma, whose plasticity-driven biology fits a persister interpretation. In bulk metastasis the up programme showed no enrichment even when adequately measured, but this null was uninformative because a bulk score dilutes a state carried by few cells.

The programme is therefore a genuine, specific and independent prognostic marker in treated colorectal cancer, of context-dependent value in other tissues, and of unproven but not disproven relevance to metastasis. This is a partial validation of the persister hypothesis, and it identifies external validation in treated cohorts and single-cell study of metastasis as the decisive next tests.
