# Abstract

**Background.** Many colorectal cancer patients relapse after chemotherapy even when their tumour first responds, and this is thought to involve drug-tolerant persister (DTP) cells: a reversible, slow-cycling state that some tumour cells enter under treatment and later exit to regrow. The Atlasi laboratory has derived a transcriptomic DTP signature from colorectal cancer cell lines treated with 5-fluorouracil. Whether such a cell-line-derived signature carries any clinical information in patient tumours was unknown. The primary aim of this thesis was to test whether the DTP signature has prognostic value in colorectal cancer patients; two secondary, exploratory questions asked whether that value extends to other cancers and to metastasis.

**Methods.** The DTP signature (up, down and composite scores) was scored in each patient tumour by single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA), together with six reference gene sets spanning stemness, regeneration and proliferation, so that any prognostic signal could be judged for biological specificity. The signature was related to overall survival and recurrence at a pre-specified three-year landmark in two independent colorectal cohorts (GSE39582, n≈585; TCGA-COAD, n=455), with adjustment for stage, microsatellite status and molecular subtype. It was then scored across 25 solid-tumour cohorts of The Cancer Genome Atlas, and tested for enrichment in a paired normal, primary and liver-metastasis dataset by differential expression and gene set enrichment analysis.

**Results.** In colorectal cancer patients, a higher DTP up score predicted worse survival and earlier recurrence. The association held in both cohorts and both platforms, was strongest in adjuvant-treated patients, and remained significant after adjustment for stage, microsatellite status and molecular subtype, so it is independent prognostic information rather than a restatement of the standard variables. It was biologically specific, tracking with a fetal-regenerative programme but not with generic stemness or proliferation, and it was elevated in the aggressive CMS4 and PDS2 subtypes. Notably it was prognostic even in tumours profiled before any chemotherapy, indicating the state is present at baseline in aggressive tumours and not created by treatment alone. In the two exploratory analyses, the signature was prognostic in only a few other cancer types (low-grade glioma and pancreatic adenocarcinoma) and weak elsewhere, and it was not enriched in bulk metastasis, though a bulk measurement cannot exclude a signal confined to a minority of cells.

**Conclusions.** A drug-tolerant persister signature derived from colorectal cancer cell lines carries genuine, independent prognostic value in colorectal cancer patients, which was the primary aim of the work. Its value in other cancer types is tissue-specific rather than general, and its role in metastasis remains open. The clearest next steps are validation in a cohort with paired pre- and post-treatment material and single-cell analysis to locate the signature within tumour cell populations.
