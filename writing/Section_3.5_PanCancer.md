## 3.5 Generalisation across cancer types (pan-cancer)

The first exploratory question is whether the prognostic value established in colorectal cancer extends to other cancer types. This is genuinely exploratory: a signature selected under 5-fluorouracil in colorectal cells has no strong reason to predict outcome in cancers with different biology and different treatments, so the analysis asks where, if anywhere, the signal recurs rather than testing a prediction that it must. It was scored in 25 solid-tumour TCGA cohorts. Within each cohort a univariable continuous-score Cox model gave a hazard ratio per one standard deviation of the score (Figure 10). A pooled analysis on the batch-corrected score, with cancer type as the batch, then tested whether the shift held across tissues; it is shown as the score distribution split by three-year outcome with a rank-biserial effect size (Figure 11), because the pooled hazard ratios lie close to one and are more clearly represented as distributions than on a forest plot. Endpoints were overall survival and recurrence, the latter the progression-free interval (PFI). Hazard ratios are per SD.

Two cohorts carried the association far more strongly and consistently than colorectal tissue itself. In low-grade glioma (TCGA-LGG, 514 patients) the up score raised hazard at both endpoints (overall survival HR 2.00 per SD, 95% CI 1.59 to 2.51, FDR < 0.001, concordance 0.70; recurrence HR 1.42, 1.21 to 1.66, FDR < 0.001), and the composite score was stronger still (overall survival HR 2.04, 1.67 to 2.48, FDR < 0.001, concordance 0.73; recurrence HR 1.56, 1.34 to 1.80, FDR < 0.001). Pancreatic adenocarcinoma (TCGA-PAAD, 178 patients) showed the same four-way consistency (up score overall survival HR 1.63, 1.26 to 2.11, FDR = 0.004, and recurrence HR 1.71, 1.34 to 2.18, FDR < 0.001; composite overall survival HR 1.47, 1.17 to 1.84, FDR = 0.013, and recurrence HR 1.54, 1.24 to 1.92, FDR = 0.002). In both tissues the up and composite scores agreed in direction and significance across both endpoints, the cleanest cross-endpoint replication anywhere in the panel, and their concordance of 0.60 to 0.73 sat well above the near-chance discrimination seen in most cohorts.

The colorectal tissue itself was weaker by comparison. In TCGA-COAD the up score reached significance for recurrence only (HR 1.34, 1.11 to 1.61, FDR = 0.029) and was borderline for overall survival (HR 1.35, 1.08 to 1.69, FDR = 0.065), while the composite and down scores were null at both endpoints (all FDR > 0.23). This matches the whole-cohort TCGA-COAD result in Section 3.2: the pan-cancer analysis pools all patients rather than isolating the adjuvant-treated subset in which the colorectal signal was strongest, so it reflects the weaker whole-cohort association rather than the treated-enriched one. So the tissue that motivated the signature carried a real but modest recurrence association, one of six tests significant, whereas glioma and pancreatic cancer, not colorectum, were where the score marked risk most decisively.

The remaining significant results were scattered across tissues and scores. For the up score, lung squamous carcinoma reached overall-survival significance (TCGA-LUSC HR 1.29, 1.08 to 1.53, FDR = 0.045). The composite score was significant in seven cohort-endpoints, adding glioblastoma at recurrence (TCGA-GBM HR 1.22, 1.08 to 1.39, FDR = 0.020) and, in the reverse direction, papillary kidney cancer at both endpoints, where a higher composite score marked better outcome (TCGA-KIRP HR 0.51, 0.34 to 0.76, FDR = 0.014 for overall survival; HR 0.61, 0.45 to 0.82, FDR = 0.015 for recurrence). The down score was significant in two cohorts, both toward higher hazard (lung adenocarcinoma overall survival TCGA-LUAD HR 1.38, 1.15 to 1.65, FDR = 0.010; cervical cancer recurrence TCGA-CESC HR 1.41, 1.12 to 1.78, FDR = 0.035). The takeaway is one of concentration rather than spread: the up score ran above one in 15 of 24 testable cohorts for overall survival and 14 of 25 for recurrence, so its modal direction matched the hypothesis, but significance clustered in a few tissues and reversed in kidney, so the programme is prognostic in particular tissue contexts rather than uniformly across the atlas. Only TCGA-TGCT overall survival failed the events gate.

Pooling all 25 cohorts on the batch-corrected score, the shift was small but present (Figure 11). The score was higher in the group with the poorer three-year outcome, with rank-biserial effect sizes of -0.079 for the up score at overall survival (FDR < 0.001) and -0.052 at recurrence (FDR 0.003), -0.059 for the down score at overall survival (FDR < 0.001), and -0.031 and -0.047 for the composite score at the two endpoints (FDR 0.041 and 0.006); only the down score at recurrence was not significant. Five of the six contrasts reached significance, but the effect sizes were near 0.03 to 0.08, so the pooled separation is detectable in a sample of this size while remaining small, as the near-complete overlap of the distributions shows.

The direction was therefore broadly consistent with a shared tendency for the up score to mark higher hazard, and in low-grade glioma and pancreatic cancer that tendency was strong, consistent across both endpoints and both scores, and backed by genuine discrimination (concordance up to 0.73). But the generalisation as a whole was uneven: significant in a minority of cohorts, reversed in kidney, small when pooled across all tissues, and of near-chance discrimination in the pool, with pooled concordance near 0.53 (Appendix). This is partial support for a cross-tissue stress programme, strongest where the signature tracks the biology of particular tumours, rather than a uniform pan-cancer prognostic effect. Two features are worth stating plainly. The per-cohort signal in glioma and pancreatic cancer far exceeds the small pooled effect, so pooling across tissues masks tissue-specific associations that are individually strong, glioma most of all. And the Down score, which was null for outcome in Section 3.2, carried a small significant pooled overall-survival shift and two per-cohort hits. Interpretation is taken up in the Discussion. Whether the programme is not merely prognostic but concentrated in the compartment where persister selection is expected, the metastasis, is the third and final prediction.
