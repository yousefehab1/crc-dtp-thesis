# 4. Discussion

## 4.1 Principal findings

This thesis set out to test a single hypothesis: that a transcriptomic drug-tolerant persister programme, if it reflects a genuine cancer-stress state rather than a colorectal-specific artefact, should make three independent and directionally consistent predictions. It should track outcome in primary colorectal cancer, generalise across cancer types, and be enriched in the metastatic compartment where persister selection is expected. The three strands returned three different verdicts. The primary colorectal prediction was supported, clearly and independently of clinicopathology. The cross-tissue prediction was supported only in part, with a strong signal in a minority of tissues and a weak one overall. The metastasis prediction was not supported at the bulk level, but for reasons that make that null uninformative rather than decisive. Throughout, the up-regulated persister component, and the Composite that follows it, carried the colorectal signal, while the down-regulated component did not. The clearest and most secure of the three results is the primary colorectal association, so the interpretation begins there.

## 4.2 A real and specific prognostic signal in colorectal cancer

The primary result is the one that can be trusted most, because it satisfies the conditions that distinguish a real association from a cohort-specific one. It replicated across two independent cohorts and two platforms, an Affymetrix microarray series and a TCGA RNA-sequencing cohort, it was clearest for recurrence and in the adjuvant-treated subset, and it survived adjustment for stage, microsatellite status and both molecular-subtype taxonomies (Section 3.4). A score that keeps its effect after adjustment for the established prognostic variables is adding information of its own rather than restating the standard taxonomy, and the persister up score did so with only modest attenuation, and with almost none at all in the adjuvant-treated subset.

The specificity of that signal is the single most important point in the thesis, and it rests on the reference panel. If any proliferation- or stemness-related score split outcome, the persister result would be unremarkable. It did not. The fetal intestinal programme tracked outcome alongside the up score, but the canonical crypt-base columnar stemness programme, the revival and regenerative stem-cell programmes, and the inflammatory bowel disease programme did not, and the MYC proliferation module ran in the opposite direction, higher in the better-outcome patients (Section 3.2). The prognostic signal therefore sits on the persister and fetal-regenerative axis specifically, not on generic stemness or on proliferation, which is precisely the comparison the panel was designed to make.

That the association was strongest in the adjuvant-treated subset fits the persister interpretation directly rather than incidentally. A drug-tolerant state is, by definition, the state that matters most under cytotoxic pressure, so a persister score should discriminate outcome best where patients have been treated, and it did (Sharma et al. 2010; Rehman et al. 2021). The convergence of the effect on treated patients is the kind of internal consistency that a merely prognostic but biologically unrelated gene list would not be expected to show.

This reading also connects to an independently derived body of work on slow-cycling colorectal biology, and the connection is worth developing rather than noting in passing. The persister and diapause state is defined by slow cycling (Sharma et al. 2010; Rehman et al. 2021), and a recent pathway-level classification of colorectal cancer identified a slow-cycling subtype, PDS3, as carrying the worst prognosis in locally advanced disease (Malla et al. 2024). Three points follow. First, the existence of PDS3 shows that slow-cycling biology is genuinely and independently prognostic in colorectal cancer, which lends external plausibility to a slow-cycling persister score also being prognostic. Second, the present analysis supports an overlap directly: the persister score varied most across the PDS axis of any taxonomy (Section 3.3), and PDS absorbed more of the overall-survival signal than either CMS or clinicopathology (Section 3.4), so the score is partly capturing the pathway-level slow-cycling and regenerative biology that PDS also measures. Third, the boundary of the claim must be held: the mapping is not one-to-one, because the persister subgroup signal was clearest in the regenerative PDS2 stratum rather than in PDS3 itself, so the relationship is one of shared slow-cycling and regenerative biology rather than identity between the score and any single subtype. This is convergent evidence from one external study, not proof, but it points the same way. A signal that is real, specific to the persister and fetal-regenerative axis, and strongest under chemotherapy in colorectal cancer raises the question of whether it marks a state shared across cancers or one peculiar to this tissue.

## 4.3 Prognostic in some tissues, not a universal stress marker

The pan-cancer result answers that question with a qualified no. Read honestly, the generalisation was weak: the direction of effect was broadly consistent with higher persister scores marking worse outcome, but statistical significance concentrated in a handful of tissues, the pooled effect across all cohorts was small, and the pooled discrimination sat close to chance (Section 3.5). A programme that were a truly universal cancer-stress marker would have produced a broad, even shift; this did not.

Where the signal was strong, however, is the informative part, and it invites a mechanistic reading. Low-grade glioma and pancreatic adenocarcinoma both showed the up and composite scores significant at both endpoints with genuine discrimination, reaching a concordance of 0.73 in glioma, above colorectal cancer itself. These are not arbitrary tissues. Both are tumours in which non-genetic plasticity and slow-cycling drug tolerance are recognised drivers rather than incidental features, which is exactly the biology a persister programme should mark. In pancreatic adenocarcinoma, therapy resistance runs substantially through epithelial-to-mesenchymal and regenerative reprogramming, and epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition has been shown to induce chemoresistance in pancreatic cancer directly (Zheng et al. 2015), the same fetal-regenerative axis on which the persister score tracked outcome in colorectal cancer. In glioma, single-cell studies show that the malignant compartment is organised as a developmental hierarchy in which stem-like and progenitor states drive growth, so prognosis is dominated by dedifferentiation and plasticity (Tirosh et al. 2016; Neftel et al. 2019), and a slow-cycling stress programme plausibly captures the more plasticity-prone, aggressive tumours. The shared thread is that in both tissues the persister and fetal-regenerative axis is a known route to aggressiveness, so a prognostic signal there is consistent with the programme marking a genuine stress state rather than tissue-specific noise.

That interpretation must be tempered by a methodological caveat specific to glioma. Low-grade glioma carries an unusually wide prognostic spread, driven by grade and by IDH-mutation status, so almost any biologically meaningful axis discriminates outcome well in that cohort. The high concordance is therefore not, on its own, evidence of a persister-specific effect, and the glioma result should be read as consistent with the mechanism rather than as proof of it. The remaining significant results reinforce the picture of context dependence rather than universality: the composite score was protective in papillary kidney cancer, reversing direction, and the down score reached significance in two further tissues (Section 3.5), so the programme is neither uniformly oriented nor uniformly prognostic across the atlas. If the programme is prognostic only in particular tissue contexts, the sharpest remaining test is not another tissue but the compartment where persister selection itself is expected, the metastasis.

## 4.4 The bulk metastasis result cannot resolve the prediction

The metastasis strand returned a null for the up programme: after the liver-contamination confound was removed, the persister up score showed no enrichment in the metastasis in either direction, so this dataset does not support the metastasis prediction at the bulk level (Section 3.6). The null is a genuine one, not an artefact: the up set was adequately covered in this dataset, scored on 74 per cent of its genes, above the 70 per cent coverage gate used throughout (Methods 2.2), so the metastasis contrast is a real test of the programme rather than the product of a partial gene set. What that genuine bulk null cannot do, however, is rule out a signal confined to a minority of cells, for the reason set up in the Introduction.

That reason is the tension between a cell-state biology and a bulk measurement. A persister state is occupied by only a fraction of cells at any one time, whether or not the capacity to enter it is broadly shared (Sharma et al. 2010; Rehman et al. 2021), and a bulk single-sample score averages over every cell in the tissue. A small paired cohort of bulk samples is therefore precisely the setting in which a real but sparse signal would be diluted below detection, so even a genuine bulk null carries little evidential weight against the hypothesis.

What did move in the metastasis is nonetheless informative. The down programme, the MYC module, and the revival and regenerative stem-cell programmes were all enriched in the metastasis, while the fetal programme only trended in that direction (Section 3.6). The metastatic compartment in bulk therefore engages proliferation and regenerative or revival stemness rather than the up-regulated persister programme, which is a coherent biological picture and a clear lead for single-cell follow-up, where the dilution that limits a bulk analysis does not apply. The credibility of the whole metastasis analysis rests on its handling of the liver confound, which was not cosmetic: adjusting the ranking for a liver-specific score changed which programmes appeared enriched and removed a spurious inflammatory enrichment that was an artefact of hepatic contamination, so the adjusted ranking is the one to trust. With one prediction supported, one partial and one unresolved for a defensible reason, the three strands can now be read together as a single qualified verdict on the hypothesis.

## 4.5 What the three predictions say together

Drawn together, the three strands describe a programme that behaves as a genuine, specific and context-limited prognostic state. It is prognostic and independent in primary colorectal cancer, strongest where cytotoxic selection is strongest; it is prognostic in a subset of other tissues whose biology matches a persister interpretation, but not across cancer in general; and it is neither confirmed nor refuted as a metastasis-seeding programme, because bulk tissue cannot resolve that question at the cell-state resolution it requires. This is a partial validation, and it is an honest one.

A single thread runs through all three strands and is the strongest positive result of the work: the persister and fetal-regenerative axis carried the prognostic weight wherever the programme was prognostic, while generic stemness and proliferation did not, and in the metastasis it was the down and revival programmes, not the up score, that moved. The reference panel therefore did its job of separating the persister signal from its biological neighbours, and the specificity it revealed is more informative than any single hazard ratio. Set against the standard a sceptic would demand, that a real state should behave differently from an artefactual gene list across independent lines of evidence, the programme clears the primary-outcome and independence bars decisively, clears the cross-tissue bar partially, and leaves the metastasis bar untested rather than failed. This reading is only as strong as the design that produced it, so its supports and its soft spots must be stated directly.

## 4.6 Strengths

Several features of the design give the positive result its weight. The primary association was replicated across two cohorts and two platforms, so it does not depend on the idiosyncrasies of one dataset or one assay. The shared TCGA-COAD cohort was processed identically wherever it appeared, in the colorectal and pan-cancer arms alike, so cross-arm comparisons are not confounded by pipeline differences. The reference panel was chosen on biological grounds to span the axes a persister result would have to be distinguished from, which is what made the specificity argument possible rather than merely asserted. The confounding and effect-modification questions were kept separate and answered with distinct models, so that independence and interaction were not conflated. And the metastasis analysis confronted its central confound directly, using a liver-specific score as a contamination proxy with a negative-control validation, rather than ignoring it. These strengths make the positive result credible, but they do not remove the limitations that make the two weaker results provisional.

## 4.7 Limitations

The limitations follow the design as closely as the strengths do. The landmark analysis trades statistical power for correct classification of early events, so the cohorts are smaller than the raw sample sizes suggest. The Cox models assume proportional hazards, which was checked but not guaranteed in every stratum. Because ssGSEA scores are relative within each sample, the comparability of scores rests on consistent normalisation across platforms, which the processing pipeline enforced but which remains an assumption. The interaction and metastasis analyses were low-powered, so their null results mark limited power rather than proven uniformity or genuine absence, and this is the key limit on the metastasis prediction, where a bulk score cannot resolve a state confined to a minority of cells. Adjustment removes only measured confounders, so residual confounding by unmeasured variables remains possible even for the independent primary result. And multiple-testing correction reduces but does not eliminate false positives. Two of these limitations are not fixed facts of the data but decisions still open in the analysis, and they should be named as such.

## 4.8 Open methodological questions

The first is the false-discovery correction family. The false-discovery rate is defined only relative to the set of tests grouped together, so the choice of family is a judgment that changes which results are called significant, and the borderline colorectal results were shown to be sensitive to it: the TCGA-COAD recurrence screen shifted from a corrected 0.032 to 0.064 when the reference panel changed, with no change in the underlying data (Section 3.2). The position taken here, fixed in Methods 2.6, is to use narrow per-stratum correction, to report both raw and corrected p-values, and to rest the confirmatory conclusion on the primary colorectal association, which survives conservative correction and does not depend on the family, while treating results near the threshold as borderline rather than positive. The interpretive point for this chapter is simply that the headline claim of the thesis rests on the confirmatory result, not on any single borderline test, so it is robust to how the family is drawn. The second open question is the input scale of the PDS classifier, a smaller technical matter of whether the classifier receives values on the scale it expects, which should be confirmed before the subtype-adjusted results are treated as final. With the evidence weighed, its strengths and soft spots set out, and the open choices named, what can and cannot be concluded from the work can now be stated plainly.
