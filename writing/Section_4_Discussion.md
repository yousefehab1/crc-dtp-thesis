# 4. Discussion

## 4.1 Principal findings

The main finding of this thesis is simple to state: a drug-tolerant persister signature derived entirely from colorectal cancer cell lines carries genuine, independent prognostic information in colorectal cancer patients. That was the primary aim, and it was met. A higher persister score marked worse survival and earlier recurrence across two independent cohorts and two platforms, and the signal survived adjustment for stage, microsatellite status and molecular subtype, so it is the tumour's own biology speaking rather than a restatement of the standard prognostic variables. Two further, exploratory questions were then asked of the same signature, and neither was a necessary consequence of the primary result. Whether the signature is prognostic in other cancer types was answered only in part: it was strong in a few tissues and weak overall, which is unsurprising for a signature selected under one drug in one cancer. Whether the programme is enriched in metastasis was not supported at the bulk level, with the important caveat that a bulk measurement cannot resolve a state confined to a minority of cells. Throughout, the up-regulated component, and the Composite that follows it, carried the colorectal signal, while the down-regulated component did not. The interpretation therefore begins with the primary and most secure result, the colorectal prognostic association.

## 4.2 A real and specific prognostic signal in colorectal cancer

The primary result is the one that can be trusted most, because it satisfies the conditions that distinguish a real association from a cohort-specific one. It replicated across two independent cohorts and two platforms, an Affymetrix microarray series and a TCGA RNA-sequencing cohort, it was clearest for recurrence and in the adjuvant-treated subset, and it survived adjustment for stage, microsatellite status and both molecular-subtype taxonomies (Section 3.4). A score that keeps its effect after adjustment for the established prognostic variables is adding information of its own rather than restating the standard taxonomy, and the persister up score did so with only modest attenuation, and with almost none at all in the adjuvant-treated subset. One qualification should be noted for that treated-subset result: because higher-stage disease is preferentially selected for adjuvant chemotherapy, stage varies less within the treated subset than in the whole cohort, so clinicopathology has less scope to absorb the score's effect there, and the near-absence of attenuation partly reflects this restricted stage range rather than independence alone.

The specificity of that signal is the single most important point in the thesis, and it rests on the reference panel. If any proliferation- or stemness-related score split outcome, the persister result would be unremarkable. It did not. The fetal intestinal programme tracked outcome alongside the up score, but the canonical crypt-base columnar stemness programme, the revival and regenerative stem-cell programmes, and the inflammatory bowel disease programme did not, and the MYC proliferation module ran in the opposite direction, higher in the better-outcome patients (Section 3.2). The prognostic signal therefore sits on the persister and fetal-regenerative axis specifically, not on generic stemness or on proliferation, which is precisely the comparison the panel was designed to make.

That the association was strongest in the adjuvant-treated subset fits the persister interpretation directly rather than incidentally. A drug-tolerant state is, by definition, the state that matters most under cytotoxic pressure, so a persister score should discriminate outcome best where patients have been treated, and it did (Sharma et al. 2010; Rehman et al. 2021). The convergence of the effect on treated patients is the kind of internal consistency that a merely prognostic but biologically unrelated gene list would not be expected to show.

There is, however, an apparent paradox that is worth confronting because its resolution strengthens rather than weakens the interpretation. The signature was defined by exposing cells to chemotherapy, yet it is prognostic in TCGA-COAD, a cohort of primary tumours resected and profiled before any adjuvant treatment (only 23 of 455 patients carry any recorded therapy). How can a chemotherapy-induced programme be elevated, and predict poor outcome, in tumours that have not yet seen chemotherapy? The most likely answer is that chemotherapy is not the only inducer of this state. A persister-like, slow-cycling transcriptional programme can also be driven by the intrinsic stresses of the tumour microenvironment, hypoxia, nutrient limitation and loss of matrix attachment, and by the mesenchymal, revival-stem-like biology of the more aggressive tumours; indeed the source study finds this same signature most enriched in the CMS4 and PDS2 subtypes (Mirzadeh Azad et al., in press). On this reading the persister score does not require prior treatment: it marks a baseline stress-tolerant and plastic cell state that is already present in aggressive, treatment-naive tumours, that predicts their poorer outcome, and that chemotherapy later selects for and amplifies. This reconciles the treated-subset and the treatment-naive results under one biology rather than two.

This reading also connects to an independently derived body of work on slow-cycling colorectal biology, and the connection is worth developing rather than noting in passing. The persister and diapause state is defined by slow cycling (Sharma et al. 2010; Rehman et al. 2021), and a recent pathway-level classification of colorectal cancer identified a slow-cycling subtype, PDS3, as carrying the worst prognosis in locally advanced disease (Malla et al. 2024). Three points follow. First, the existence of PDS3 shows that slow-cycling biology is genuinely and independently prognostic in colorectal cancer, which lends external plausibility to a slow-cycling persister score also being prognostic. Second, the present analysis supports an overlap directly: the persister score varied most across the PDS axis of any taxonomy (Section 3.3), and PDS absorbed more of the overall-survival signal than either CMS or clinicopathology (Section 3.4), so the score is partly capturing the pathway-level slow-cycling and regenerative biology that PDS also measures. Third, the boundary of the claim must be held: the mapping is not one-to-one, because the persister subgroup signal was clearest in the regenerative PDS2 stratum rather than in PDS3 itself, so the relationship is one of shared slow-cycling and regenerative biology rather than identity between the score and any single subtype. This is convergent evidence from one external study, not proof, but it points the same way. A signal that is real, specific to the persister and fetal-regenerative axis, and strongest under chemotherapy in colorectal cancer raises the question of whether it marks a state shared across cancers or one peculiar to this tissue.

## 4.3 Prognostic in some tissues, not a universal stress marker

The pan-cancer analysis provided only partial support for generalisation. The direction of effect was broadly consistent with higher persister scores marking worse outcome, but statistical significance was confined to a minority of tissues, the pooled effect across all cohorts was small, and the pooled discrimination lay close to chance (Section 3.5). A truly universal cancer-stress marker would be expected to produce a broad and even shift across cohorts, which was not observed.

Where the signal was strong, however, is the informative part, and it invites a mechanistic reading. Low-grade glioma and pancreatic adenocarcinoma both showed the up and composite scores significant at both endpoints with genuine discrimination, reaching a concordance of 0.73 in glioma, above colorectal cancer itself. These are not arbitrary tissues. Both are tumours in which non-genetic plasticity and slow-cycling drug tolerance are recognised drivers rather than incidental features, which is exactly the biology a persister programme should mark. In pancreatic adenocarcinoma, therapy resistance runs substantially through epithelial-to-mesenchymal and regenerative reprogramming, and epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition has been shown to induce chemoresistance in pancreatic cancer directly (Zheng et al. 2015), the same fetal-regenerative axis on which the persister score tracked outcome in colorectal cancer. In glioma, single-cell studies show that the malignant compartment is organised as a developmental hierarchy in which stem-like and progenitor states drive growth, so prognosis is dominated by dedifferentiation and plasticity (Tirosh et al. 2016; Neftel et al. 2019), and a slow-cycling stress programme plausibly captures the more plasticity-prone, aggressive tumours. The shared thread is that in both tissues the persister and fetal-regenerative axis is a known route to aggressiveness, so a prognostic signal there is consistent with the programme marking a genuine stress state rather than tissue-specific noise.

That interpretation must be tempered by a methodological caveat specific to glioma. Low-grade glioma carries an unusually wide prognostic spread, driven by grade and by IDH-mutation status, so almost any biologically meaningful axis discriminates outcome well in that cohort. The high concordance is therefore not, on its own, evidence of a persister-specific effect, and the glioma result should be read as consistent with the mechanism rather than as proof of it. The remaining significant results reinforce the picture of context dependence rather than universality: the composite score was protective in papillary kidney cancer, reversing direction, and the down score reached significance in two further tissues (Section 3.5), so the programme is neither uniformly oriented nor uniformly prognostic across the atlas. If the programme is prognostic only in particular tissue contexts, the sharpest remaining test is not another tissue but the compartment where persister selection itself is expected, the metastasis.

## 4.4 The metastasis prediction is not supported at the bulk level

The metastasis strand did not support the third prediction. After the liver-contamination confound was removed, the persister up programme showed no enrichment in the metastasis, and it was equally null in the earlier normal-to-primary transition (Section 3.6). Because the up set was adequately covered in this dataset, scored on 74 per cent of its genes and above the coverage gate of Methods 2.2, this is a genuine negative result at the bulk level rather than a coverage artefact. Taken at face value, the data do not show the up programme to be differentially abundant in the metastatic compartment, and this gap between the model and the data should be stated plainly.

What did move is informative in its own right. Across both transitions the down programme was enriched alongside the MYC module and the revival and regenerative stem-cell programmes, with the fetal programme also enriched in the metastasis (Section 3.6). The primary and metastatic compartments in bulk therefore engage proliferation and regenerative stemness rather than the up-regulated persister programme, so the biology is coherent even though it does not match the prediction.

The behaviour of the down score in particular now has a coherent explanation. An over-representation analysis of the down set (Gene Ontology biological process) showed it is dominated by cytoplasmic translation and ribosome-biogenesis genes (the strongest term, cytoplasmic translation, at an adjusted p of 1e-28). This is the protein-synthesis machinery that slow-cycling, diapause-like persisters characteristically repress (Rehman et al. 2021), so the down set is best understood not as a second persister axis but as its opposite: a high down score marks biosynthetically active, proliferative, non-persister cells. That single interpretation ties together the otherwise disconnected places the down score appeared. It was enriched in the metastasis, where outgrowth demands high translational activity; it marked worse outcome in the proliferative lung and cervical cohorts (Section 3.5); and it was null for outcome in primary colorectal cancer (Section 3.2), because a proliferation and translation axis adds little there. It also explains the design of the composite score: subtracting the down (translation) axis from the up (stress) axis sharpens the specifically persister signal, which is why the composite tracked the up score throughout rather than the down.

The most likely reconciliation is a limitation of the measurement rather than a refutation of the hypothesis, but this should be offered as a caveat, not as a way of discounting the null. A persister state is occupied by only a fraction of cells at any one time (Sharma et al. 2010; Rehman et al. 2021), and a bulk score averages over the whole tissue, so a signal confined to a small subpopulation could go undetected in a bulk paired design of this size. This can be made quantitative rather than left as "underpowered": for a paired design with about eighteen patients, the minimum standardised effect detectable at 80% power and a two-sided 5% level is approximately 0.66, a medium-to-large shift, so only a substantial, tissue-wide difference could have been detected, and a diluted subpopulation signal is exactly what would be missed. Computational deconvolution of the bulk profiles was considered as a partial remedy but not pursued, because without a reference expression profile for the persister state itself, deconvolution can estimate broad cell-type fractions but cannot isolate a rare within-epithelial persister subpopulation; it is therefore left, with single-cell profiling, for future work. That the cell-state question remains open is a legitimate reason to seek higher-resolution measurement (Section 5.2); it does not, however, turn the bulk null into support for the prediction. The honest position is that the metastasis prediction is unsupported in the data available, and that determining whether this reflects a true absence or a diluted subpopulation signal will require higher-resolution measurement.

The credibility of the analysis rests on its handling of the liver confound: adjusting the ranking for a liver-specific score changed which programmes appeared enriched and removed a spurious inflammatory enrichment driven by hepatic contamination, so the adjusted ranking is the one reported. With one prediction supported, one partial and one unsupported, the three strands can now be read together.

## 4.5 What the three predictions say together

Drawn together, the three strands describe a programme that behaves as a genuine, specific and context-limited prognostic state. It is prognostic and independent in primary colorectal cancer, strongest where cytotoxic selection is strongest; it is prognostic in a subset of other tissues whose biology matches a persister interpretation, but not across cancer in general; and the prediction that it is enriched in the metastatic compartment was unsupported in the bulk data available, leaving open, but not answered, whether a signal confined to a cell subpopulation exists. This is a partial validation, and it is an honest one.

A single thread runs through all three strands and is the strongest positive result of the work: the persister and fetal-regenerative axis carried the prognostic weight wherever the programme was prognostic, while generic stemness and proliferation did not, and in the metastasis it was the down and revival programmes, not the up score, that moved. The reference panel therefore did its job of separating the persister signal from its biological neighbours, and the specificity it revealed is more informative than any single hazard ratio. Set against the standard a sceptic would demand, that a real state should behave differently from an artefactual gene list across independent lines of evidence, the programme clears the primary-outcome and independence bars decisively, clears the cross-tissue bar partially, and does not clear the metastasis bar in the bulk data, with the cell-state question left open for higher-resolution work. This reading is only as strong as the design that produced it, so its supports and its soft spots must be stated directly.

## 4.6 Strengths

Several features of the design give the positive result its weight. The primary association was replicated across two cohorts and two platforms, so it does not depend on the idiosyncrasies of one dataset or one assay. The shared TCGA-COAD cohort was processed identically wherever it appeared, in the colorectal and pan-cancer arms alike, so cross-arm comparisons are not confounded by pipeline differences. The reference panel was chosen on biological grounds to span the axes a persister result would have to be distinguished from, which is what made the specificity argument possible rather than merely asserted. The confounding and effect-modification questions were kept separate and answered with distinct models, so that independence and interaction were not conflated. And the metastasis analysis confronted its central confound directly, using a liver-specific score as a contamination proxy with a negative-control validation, rather than ignoring it. These strengths make the positive result credible, but they do not remove the limitations that make the two weaker results provisional.

## 4.7 Limitations

Several limitations should be stated plainly. First, sample size limits much of the analysis. The 36-month landmark improves the accuracy of the outcome labels but discards patients censored before three years, so the effective cohorts are smaller than the totals suggest. More importantly, once the cohorts are split into subtypes, the within-subgroup survival tests, and the confounding and interaction Cox models built on them, rest on small strata with few events, so many of these tests are underpowered and their null results reflect limited power rather than a true absence of effect. Second, the metastasis analysis was based on a single small paired cohort in which a bulk score cannot resolve a state confined to a minority of cells. Third, the Cox models assume proportional hazards, which was checked but not guaranteed in every stratum, and because ssGSEA scores are relative within each sample they depend on consistent normalisation across platforms. Finally, adjustment can only remove measured confounders, so residual confounding remains possible even for the primary result, and multiple-testing correction reduces but does not eliminate false positives. A further limitation is one of benchmarking: the score was not compared head-to-head against the validated clinical prognostic assays already used in colorectal cancer, such as Oncotype DX Colon and ColoPrint (Salazar et al. 2011), or against stage and microsatellite-status nomograms. It is presented here as a mechanistic, biologically motivated signal that is complementary to, rather than a replacement for, those tools, and a formal comparison of added prognostic value remains to be done.

## 4.8 Open methodological questions

The first is the false-discovery correction family. Because the false-discovery rate is defined relative to the set of tests grouped together, deciding which tests form a family is a deliberate methodological choice rather than a flaw, and this thesis adopts a considered one: narrow per-stratum correction, with both raw and corrected p-values reported (Methods 2.6). This choice matters only at the margin, for results sitting near the 0.05 threshold, and the confirmatory conclusion, the primary colorectal association and its independence, is significant under conservative correction and does not depend on how the family is drawn. A sensitivity analysis bears this out at the other extreme: pooling the correction across all 159 testable subgroup tests, the most conservative option, leaves only the strongest subgroup association standing (Section 3.3), which is why those subgroup results are presented as exploratory while the confirmatory primary result, corrected in its own right, is not affected. The headline claim therefore rests on the robust result, not on any borderline test. The second question is a technical check on the pathway-derived subtype classifier: confirming that it receives expression values on the scale it was designed for, so that the subtype calls, and the subtype-adjusted results that depend on them, are correct. Neither point undermines the main findings; both are noted for completeness. With the evidence weighed and these choices set out, what can and cannot be concluded from the work can now be stated plainly.
